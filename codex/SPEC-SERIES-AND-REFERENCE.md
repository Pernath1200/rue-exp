# Spec — unit series, in-unit links, and the Reference guides

**Status: SPEC ONLY. Nothing built. Do not action from this file.**
Drafted 2026-09-05 with James. Choices marked **(James)** are his; everything
else is a proposal with a stated default, to be argued with rather than obeyed.

## 1. What this is for

Most units are part of a series — Articles 1/2/3, Modals 1/2/3, Travel 1/2/3.
Today a student inside `a2_articles` has no way to know `a1_articles` exists,
that a third is coming, or where the whole system is written down in one place.
This spec covers three joined things:

1. an explicit series model in the data,
2. links between units of a series, shown in the unit,
3. a general guide per series in Reference, printable.

## 2. What already exists

| Thing | State |
|---|---|
| `category` + `category_seq` on nodes | **Live, one category only.** `time_and_tenses`, 17 units, ordered 1 (a1_present_simple) to 17 (b1_future), spanning A1/A2/B1. Its note already anticipates siblings: "conditionals/wishes/reporting are future sibling categories." |

| Print / PDF | Nothing. |

## 3. Series model

**(James): extend `category` to everything — one mechanism, `related` folded in.**

- Every live unit gets a `category` and a `category_seq`. `time_and_tenses` is the
  worked example and needs no change.
- The `categories` registry in `tree.json` grows one entry per series:
  `{ label, note, level_span, reference_tab }`.
- Series are **cross-level by design**. Tenses already runs A1 to B1; Articles
  will run A1, A2, B2. A series is not a level's property.

### Series cannot be derived from unit ids — they must be authored

Worth recording because it looks derivable and is not. A naive stem match over
the 191 live nodes finds 33 series covering 77 units and calls the other 114
singletons. That is an artefact: the real Modals series is `a2_modals_must_should`,
`a2_have_to`, `a2_could_able`, `b1_modals_deduction`, `b2_modal_perfect` — no
shared stem anywhere. `time_and_tenses` proves the same point, holding
`a2_used_to`, `a2_for_since` and `a2_arrangements` together under no common name.
So: **authored field, not a regex.** Budget it as a real pass over ~191 nodes.

### Two problems this choice creates, with proposed defaults

**(a) A unit in two series.** One `category` field cannot express it, and the
cases are real: `b1_prepositions_time_2` belongs to Prepositions *and* to Time;
`b1_be_used_to` sits at tenses seq 16 and also under Used to.

*Default:* `category` is the unit's **home** series — exactly one, the one whose
Reference guide it belongs in. Secondary membership is expressed by `related`
(below). Revisit only if the count of genuinely dual units exceeds about 10.

**(b) 79 nodes of `related` data.** "Folded in" should not mean deleted.

*Default:* after categories are assigned, each `related` entry is triaged —

- points to a unit in the **same** category: redundant, delete it, the chain now
  carries it;
- points to a unit in a **different** category: **keep it.** This is the
  cross-series link and nothing else expresses it. `a1_frequency` to
  `a2_adverbs_order`, and `a1_word_order` to `b1_word_order_fronting`, are both
  this kind.

So `related` survives narrowly, as cross-category pointers only, and finally gets
rendered. If James wants it gone outright, say so — but the cross-links are
authored judgment that would have to be re-made somewhere.

## 4. In-unit links

Not asked about; proposed, so there is something to reject.

- **In the unit header:** `Articles 2 of 3` — position in the series, always
  visible, drawn from `category_seq` and the series length. `app.js` already
  builds most of this string.
- **At the end of the unit,** a small strip: previous and next in series (by
  seq), any cross-series `related` units, and one link to the series' Reference
  guide.
- **Not on the intro cards.** The intro is the teaching moment; navigation there
  competes with it.
- Links point at units regardless of level or lock state. The path is soft and
  all levels are open (`spine.json`), so there is nothing to gate.

## 5. Reference guides

**(James): one guide per series. Made of consolidated tables + rules, and links
to the units in the series. Not written prose. Not stitched-together intro cards.**

- Each series with a `reference_tab` gets a tab. A guide holds:
  1. **the whole system on one page** — the tables and rules the series teaches,
     consolidated, in the existing Reference idiom (tables + untracked drills,
     no fruit, no SRS);
  2. **the series index** — every unit in the series in seq order with a line on
     what it covers, linking into the unit. The guide is how you navigate the
     series, and the series links back to the guide.
- **Level sections stay.** James chose the B1 answer about *register*, not about
  structure, and did not choose "drop the level sections." The `verbs` tab is the
  model: one topic, sections A1 / A2 / B1 / B1+, one page.
- Generated by extending `codex/scripts/gen_reference.py`. `data/reference.json`
  stays a build output — never hand-edited.
- `spell` and `nums` are empty shells today; either fill them as part of this or
  drop them from the tab list, but do not ship a third empty tab.

### Register: pitched at B1 (James)

The explanations are written in **B1 English** — readable by an intermediate
student with no teacher present. Content still covers A1 upward; it is the prose
that is levelled, not the coverage.

*Proposal:* make this checkable rather than a matter of taste. The Oxford level
data behind `codex/scripts/rue_oxford.py` and `OXFORD-REMEASURE.md` already
grades vocabulary, and `lint.py` already runs a `vocablevel` check with an
`is_grammar` exemption. A `refregister` check over guide prose — flag content
words above B1, allow a named list of grammar metalanguage (*participle*,
*auxiliary*, *countable*) — would hold the bar without anyone re-reading
everything by hand. Same machinery, new target.

## 6. Print and PDF

**(James): the reference table, print-styled · a teacher handout · PDF, not
browser print.** Two outputs, two audiences.

**Student sheet.** The guide's tables, laid out for a page. Print CSS is the
layout source of truth; the PDF is rendered from that same HTML, so there is one
layout to maintain, not two.

**Teacher handout.** Built for the room, not the app — so it belongs on the
`Documents/classes/` path, not inside rue-exp's student-facing output. Open
question: same content differently laid out, or genuinely different content
(answer keys, teaching notes, what to watch for)? Not decided.

**Generation.** Render HTML to PDF with a headless browser print-to-PDF, which
keeps hyperlinks intact. Do **not** route this through LibreOffice: headless
docx-to-pdf drops hyperlinks, and the PyMuPDF re-add plus `check_links.py` dance
exists to repair exactly that. Rendering the HTML directly avoids the problem
rather than fixing it afterwards.

**Naming.** Anything landing on the Desktop or in `classes/` must follow the
filing convention or `filer.py` cannot route it —
`Spec_<Topic>_<Level>_<CODE>_<DATE>.md` and siblings. A sheet named
`articles_ref.pdf` becomes an UNROUTED line.

## 7. Open questions

1. Does `related` survive as cross-series links (proposed) or get deleted outright?
2. The full category list — what the series actually are. Requires a pass over
   191 live nodes and is the bulk of the work.
3. Teacher handout: reformatted student sheet, or its own content with answer keys?
4. Do `spell` and `nums` get filled or dropped?
5. Does every unit belong to a series, or are true singletons allowed a null
   category? (Proposed: allowed, and expected to be rare.)

## 8. Not in scope

Fruit, SRS or progress on Reference — it stays untracked by decision (James,
2026-08-17). No gating of links by level. No change to path order.
